Commercial identity: three directions

A study to replace "Caixa", which collides with Caixa Econômica Federal, and "Finanças", too generic to register. Nothing here is applied to the app. Audience, message and criteria are in brief.md. The first name of each direction is the one drawn; app copy stays in Portuguese.

Abrigo

The household as a shelter: a roof over a coin, the money the house keeps together.

Calm and domestic; the money of the house, not of a bank.

Names

  1. TetoFour letters, one stress in both languages; "teto" is the roof and also the ceiling of a budget.Risk: "Teto de gastos" is a common budget term, so the word alone is weak as a mark; register it with a figurative element.
  2. VarandaWarm, Brazilian, said the same in English; the place where a couple talks about the month.Risk: Used by restaurants and real estate; check class 36 (finance) and 9/42 (software).
  3. AlpendreRare word, distinctive, a covered porch; the most registrable of the three.Risk: Harder to spell for English speakers; the "dre" ending is unfamiliar abroad.

A dois

Two circles and the space they share: each member keeps a part, the overlap is the joint budget.

Shared and fair; decisions made together, Claude as the third voice that only proposes.

Names

  1. Ambos"Both" in Portuguese, two syllables, easy in English; it says the product is for the two of them.Risk: A common word: check class 36 and 9/42, and whether an app store listing already uses it.
  2. Comum"Conta comum" is how Brazilians name a joint account; reads as "common" in English.Risk: Generic in Portuguese, so the mark depends on the logo; the .com.br may be taken.
  3. DuetoTwo voices in one piece; friendly, short, close to "duet" in English.Risk: "Duo" brands are many (Duolingo, Duo Security); keep the full word.

Prumo

The plumb line: the month stays upright when every bill has its place; "estar no prumo" is to be on track.

Trustworthy and exact, numbers first; the closest to the current identity, with a deeper ink blue.

Names

  1. PrumoFive letters, a known idiom ("no prumo"), sounds solid in English.Risk: Prumo Logística (ports) exists in Brazil; another class, but check class 36 and 9/42 carefully.
  2. RemansoStill water after the current; calm, distinctive, no finance use the agent knows of.Risk: Three syllables, less obvious meaning for English speakers.
  3. SerenoCalm and also the evening dew; positive, short.Risk: Common name for hotels and products; weak alone as a mark.
